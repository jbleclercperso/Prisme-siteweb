The best software to organize a porn collection

An adult video collection grows fast: thousands of files with meaningless names, duplicates everywhere, catch-all folders, and the wish that nobody else ever stumbles on it. Several tools promise to bring order to it. They do very different things. Here is which ones, who they suit, and what each asks of you.

In short

Stash is the free reference: a server that catalogs every scene (performers, studios, tags) and fetches its metadata online. Powerful, but you install and run it like a server. Plex and Jellyfin play everything but aren't built for this content.

Prisme is a Windows app you install and that just works. It is built to sort fast, with the keyboard, into real folders, to watch with previews, and to stay discreet. It doesn't fetch metadata online.

Three kinds of tools

  • Dedicated catalogs (Stash, Porn-Vault): a database of your scenes, browsed in your web browser, with detailed metadata.
  • General media servers (Plex, Jellyfin, Emby, Kodi): built for movies and TV shows, repurposed for this content.
  • Desktop apps (Prisme): an application you open on your PC, working directly on your folders.

There are also download automation tools (Whisparr, from the Sonarr / Radarr family) and VR tools (XBVR). They answer other needs.

Stash, if you want a true catalog

Stash is the most active open-source project. It scans your collection, identifies each scene by its fingerprint, and pulls its metadata (title, performers, studio, date, tags) from community databases. You then browse it like a private site, in your browser. If you want to know everything about every video and search by performer or studio, it's the tool. The trade-off: it's a server. You have to run it, configure scanning, generate previews (slow on a big collection) and maintain it, and it sorts in its database, not in your folders. See our Stash alternative page.

Plex and Jellyfin, if you mostly watch on TV

They play everything and have good TV apps, but they don't recognize this content and don't help you sort it. Keeping a library private also takes some care. The details are in Plex or Jellyfin for porn.

Prisme, if your problem is the mess

Prisme starts from another observation: most collections don't need metadata, they need sorting. You open a folder and the previews of each video play on hover. One key files the video in the right category, and the next one shows up instantly. An hour is enough to sort what had been piling up for months.

  • Sort: up to thirty categories, one key each, a radial menu on right-click, every action undoable (details).
  • Watch: previews on hover, the Wall of two to ten videos, the floating player (details).
  • Find: keywords drawn automatically from file names, and scene search by description with an AI that runs on your PC (details).
  • Clean up: duplicates, even at another resolution (details), and repair of damaged videos.
  • Stay discreet: one gesture and everything vanishes behind a dull screen, a PIN code, hidden folders (details).
What Prisme doesn't do
  • It doesn't fetch metadata online: no performer or studio database. It works with your file names and the content of the images.
  • It doesn't provide or host any content: it's software for your own files.
  • It runs on Windows, not yet on Mac or Linux.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best porn organizer?

It depends on what you're missing. For a detailed catalog with performers and studios, Stash (free, runs as a server). To sort fast into real folders, watch with previews and stay discreet, a desktop app like Prisme.

Is there free software to manage adult videos?

Yes: Stash and Porn-Vault are free and open source, and Jellyfin can serve as a media library. Prisme has a 14-day free trial, then it's paid.

Do these tools send anything online?

Stash queries online databases for metadata, if you enable it. Plex goes through an online account. Prisme is fully local: no account, no telemetry, and its AI search runs on your PC.
